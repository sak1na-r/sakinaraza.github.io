Research

National High School Journal of Science — Lead Student Researcher & First Author
Primary research, as first author, conducted alongside a PhD mentor from Carnegie Mellon University modeling robotic photothermal evaporators for solar-driven water purification. Invited for revision by the National High School Journal of Science for lead publication.

This computational study models and simulates light-to-vapor conversion across three distinct material architectures (copper sulfide plasmonic membranes, wood-polypyrrole composites, and light-propelled photocatalytic robotic evaporators) to address non-standardized reporting in solar desalination.

Utilizing Python (NumPy) for spectral irradiance and material absorbance modeling, alongside ImageJ for microstructural pore-geometry analysis, the study investigates the relationship between optical energy conversion and volumetric evaporation coefficients. Findings demonstrate that photothermal efficiency and evaporation coefficients operate as physically decoupled parameters (R2 = 0.01), proving that photothermal efficiency alone is a statistically insignificant predictor of overall vapor generation performance.
